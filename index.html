02 / Motion & evaluation

Robot Mount &
Trajectory Evaluation

A pipeline from recorded tasks to executable motion, with strict inverse kinematics, collision-aware branch selection, and dynamics-limited retiming.

The Fold Box video shows a MuJoCo simulation with calibrated tool-center-point paths overlaid on the executed motion.

Methods & scope

The broader evaluation covered 27 trajectories from 12 task families and 4 mount layouts; those counts do not describe this single Fold Box run. The pipeline uses Python, URDF models, SE(3) IK, collision checks, and synchronized MuJoCo video.

03 / Control & robustness

Reliable Manipulation:
PID vs Fuzzy-PID

This torque-aware simulation compares three controllers: manual PID, Mamdani Fuzzy-PID, and optimization PID. It tests where each succeeds—and where all three fail.

39 deterministic runs
360 paired stochastic trials

Results & limitations

Under combined stress, each controller succeeded in 0/30 trials. The study uses inverse-dynamics feedforward, Bayesian tuning, robustness analysis, and independent physical-model cross-validation in MATLAB, Simulink, and Simscape Multibody. Hardware performance has not been established.

Game-Diffusion-based Multi-Agent Learning of Ramp Merging Coordination for Connected and Automated Vehicles

About Me

Prospective MS / PhD applicant in robotics and AI

From robot design to measurable results.

I’m Zhenghao (Daniel) Yu, a Computer Engineering undergraduate at The University of Hong Kong. My work spans robot design and control, autonomous driving, embedded systems, and human-computer interaction. Across these areas, I focus on how systems behave beyond ideal test conditions.

At Ragtime Lab, I curated multimodal factory data, compared robot designs, evaluated mounting and trajectories, and studied manipulator control. Earlier projects took me from adversarial tests for highway merging to wheel-leg hospital navigation and contactless interfaces for noisy vehicle cabins.

I want my work to hold up when others look closely: data they can inspect, experiments they can repeat, and results that show where a system works—and where it doesn’t.
