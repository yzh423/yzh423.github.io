02 / Motion & evaluation

Robot Mount &
Trajectory Evaluation

From task registration to executable motion: a reproducible pipeline for strict inverse kinematics, collision-aware branch selection, and dynamics-limited retiming.

Shown here: Fold Box in MuJoCo. This is simulation evidence, with calibrated tool-center-point tracking visualized along the trajectory.

Methods & scope

The broader evaluation portfolio covers 27 trajectories, 12 task families, and 4 mount layouts. These are portfolio-wide counts, not the scope of this single Fold Box demonstration. The pipeline combines Python, URDF models, SE(3) IK, collision checks, and synchronized MuJoCo evidence.

03 / Control & robustness

Reliable Manipulation:
PID vs Fuzzy-PID

A torque-aware simulation study comparing three controllers: manual PID, Mamdani Fuzzy-PID, and optimization PID. The question is where each controller remains reliable—and where all three fail.

39 deterministic runs
360 paired stochastic trials

Results & limitations

All three controllers share a 0/30 success result under combined stress. The study uses inverse-dynamics feedforward, Bayesian tuning, robustness analysis, and independent physical-model cross-validation in MATLAB, Simulink, and Simscape Multibody. These simulation results do not establish hardware performance.

About Me

Prospective MS / PhD applicant in robotics and AI

Building reliable embodied intelligence.

I am Zhenghao (Daniel) Yu, a Computer Engineering undergraduate at The University of Hong Kong. My work spans robot system design, learning-based control, autonomous driving, embedded systems, and human-computer interaction. I am interested in systems that remain measurable and useful outside a clean lab setting.

Most recently, at Ragtime Lab, I worked across the robotics evaluation stack: curating multimodal factory data, comparing robot designs, optimizing dual-arm mounting and trajectory execution, and building reproducible control studies. This systems perspective complements my earlier research in safety-critical driving, wheel-leg navigation, and contactless interaction for harsh vehicle cabins.

I like research that ends in auditable evidence: documented datasets, reproducible experiments, explicit acceptance criteria, and results that state their limits as clearly as their gains.
