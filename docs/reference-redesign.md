# Main-site academic redesign

## Direction

The homepage introduces Zhenghao YU (Daniel) to prospective supervisors and collaborators. The portrait, two-line name and contact links lead into the cover illustration, with the research introduction below. An original two-dimensional engraving shows articulated parts, etched surfaces and copper accents during pickup, disassembly and reassembly. Independent particle lettering stays in front. Newsreader headings, Inter body text, thin rules, and an orange accent connect the introduction to the research and publications below. [Design reference](https://tanzhouhu.github.io/#research)

## Robot arm banner

The cover plays at 1.18 speed, giving the 8.7-second source an approximately 7.4-second viewing duration. All timings below are media-clock seconds: the engraving, name and numerical field remain synchronized. The assembly advances nearly linearly with short softened endpoints, and peripheral drift eases in after the signature settles.

The cover is an original two-dimensional mechanical engraving on white paper. The parallel gripper closes at 0.70 media seconds, lifts the bushing clear by 1.45 seconds, and separates from 1.70 to 3.30 seconds. Overlapping assembly steps return the parts from the base upward between 3.45 and 6.00 seconds. Adjacent groups start 0.32 seconds apart and travel for approximately 0.58 seconds; separation and return share near-uniform speed over their central 88%, with the same short endpoint ramps. Carrying motion, reframing and the engraved light begin at 5.72 seconds, overlapping the last gripper assembly. Ink starts departing at 5.94 seconds with short adjacent releases and no fixed delay, flowing into the two-line signature before settling by 8.62 seconds. The complete drawing retains its etched detail as it moves beside the name on desktop, or below it on smaller screens. The 8.7-second film plays at 1.18×; project recordings retain their source speed.

The 1440 × 640, 60 fps H.264 engraving and its two posters are generated by `scripts/render-grasp.py`. The video is the master clock for the drawing, numeric field and interactive name particles, including pause, seeking and replay. Per-frame silhouette bounds keep the complete drawing within the banner and separate from the signature and controls. No WebGL renderer or camera-rotation controls load on the homepage. Reduced motion shows the final drawing and name. The earlier 3D experiment remains in the repository history and archived renderer sources.

The numeric backdrop is sampled from 105 silhouettes of the same original 2D arm, exported by `scripts/render-engraving-atlas.py` as a local WebP atlas. Adjacent silhouettes interpolate at the film clock. Offset impressions open briefly, then a 4.8× bearing detail fills the character field. A short defocus bridges that close-up to an aligned negative print in green; the paper stays white at the edges. The field returns to the assembled arm and converges at the gripper before the signature. Colour follows these material changes rather than an unrelated circular backdrop. Background strength falls by 76% during the name transfer, with further attenuation behind the letters. Only restrained drift, pointer response and a slow colour cycle remain afterward. Pause, offscreen suspension and reduced motion stop automatic movement.

The pause control and reduced-motion preference provide a settled composition. Backgrounding the page pauses playback, and data saver suppresses automatic film playback. The still poster and fallback name are readable before enhancement.

## Research network

Four branches connect the central research label to eight topics. Solid lines join each parent and child, while three dashed lines show related branches and topics. The same SVG connections track the moving labels on desktop and in the taller phone layout. Topic links keep fixed hit areas. A visitor can scroll or pinch zoom over the map.

Each topic is a native link to the associated project, research row, paper or study section. Hover or focus previews the topic and highlights its path to the central junction. The entire note opens the same destination; one tap or Enter on the topic is enough. Arrow keys move between topics, and Escape dismisses the note. With JavaScript disabled, the topics form a readable grid of links.

## Research rows and scrolling

The four indexed rows have original line drawings of a robot arm, a highway merge, a mobile robot and an in-cabin interface. Hover or keyboard focus reveals a margin rule and sketch. Clicking or tapping anywhere on a research row follows its title link. Project and publication titles and lead images also open their resources directly. Each new reading unit fades in once as it enters the viewport. Motion preferences, keyboard navigation, print and script-free reading keep the content available.

## Project recordings

Still figures introduce all three homepage projects. Under the manipulation study and the motion-evaluation project, closed native disclosures hold the path-tracking simulation and the Fold Box trajectory replay. Opening a disclosure exposes the player and caption. The reader presses Play to start the recording, and closing its disclosure pauses it. The separate manipulation study page leads with its given-path player and offers the matched endpoint-only recording on demand. Captions distinguish simulation from hardware and identify the protocol behind each result.
